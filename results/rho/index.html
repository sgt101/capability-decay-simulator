What these correlations are telling us

The correlation answers one specific question: when a report says “condition A produced a bigger capability loss than condition B”, does that claim survive if ρ were smaller than 1000 — or was it only ever true because ρ = 1000 weights the top of the distribution so heavily?

That is narrower than “does ρ matter”. The magnitudes plainly move: the median change shifts by double digits across the sweep. Correlation is blind to magnitude — it asks only whether the order of cells is preserved. A config can have a wildly ρ-sensitive effect size and a rock-solid ranking.

Where it is high the comparative claims rest on the mechanism, not the assumption: whatever is said about “this cell beats that cell” would still hold at ρ = 8 instead of 1000.

Where it is low or negative the ranking itself is contingent on ρ. That is stronger than “the numbers are noisy”: a negative correlation means the ordering reverses, so the cell that looks worst at the shipped ρ is close to the one that looked best at the bottom of the range. Any claim of the form “X is most damaging when Y” drawn from such a set needs stating as a claim about ρ = 1000 specifically, not about the mechanism.

Why the split falls where it does. Pairings that act by shifting the whole expertise distribution — dampening, or reshaping peer pools through M — move everyone’s E together, so reweighting the sum by ρ mostly rescales and the ranking survives. The assisted-learning pairings encode a leverage term that treats different bands of the distribution asymmetrically, by where each person sits relative to the AI’s level. Which cell “wins” then depends on exactly where ρ concentrates the sum, and that is a different question cell by cell — which is why the ordering can invert rather than merely rescale.
