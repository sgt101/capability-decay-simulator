Where AI is a net gain in capability

acl.2.json · tick 1440 · generated 2026-09-26

Does AI leave the system with more capability than it had without? The answer turns on three numbers from one study of one profession — Novice Gain (gn), Expert Gain (gx) and Novice Deficit (dn) — and on expert value — what one person in the top 1% of the field is worth in threshold experts, swept from ×26 to ×202 (ρ 60 to 800). All four are controls below, not settings.

18 cells × 32 replicates, expertise snapshotted at tick 1440 · axes aiLevelFraction x aiDampeningBelow · θ = 0.585 · frontier breadth 1 · blend sharpness 20 · 25 log-spaced values of ρ from 60 to 800 · no simulation was run to draw this

What the four numbers are
  • Novice Gain (aclNoviceGain) — output AI adds for weaker performers, on work inside its frontier.
  • Expert Gain (aclExpertGain) — the same for stronger performers. Smaller: AI levels the field rather than lifting it evenly.
  • Novice Deficit (aclNoviceDeficit) — output lost outside the frontier, by people who cannot tell the AI's work is wrong.
  • Expert value — the control is w at E = 0.914, the 99th percentile of this run's own no-AI population, and ρ follows from ρ = w(1−θ)/(E−θ). One number shown two ways; both are on every readout, since ρ is what the rest of the study and every CSV column is keyed to. w is not a single multiple for everyone: at ×202 for the top 1%, a person at the median counts 1.6 and the expert threshold counts 1. Note also that ρ itself is never realised — w(1) = ρ describes an E = 1 nobody in this population reaches. A stated assumption, never measured, which is why it is swept.

None of the four touches a career — they are read off the population at the end of the run. That is why one run answers for three of the published configs at once, and why no simulation was needed to draw this page. Plotted values are medians across this experiment's 18 cells, each against its own no-AI arm, from the same seeds. The treatment arm has already lost expertise to AI-dampened learning before any leverage applies; above zero means the leverage earned that back.
