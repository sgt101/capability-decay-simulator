Where AI is a net gain in capability

acl.5.json · tick 1440 · generated 2026-09-26

System capability is C = Σ w(E) ℓ(E): each person's expertise converted into threshold-expert equivalents by w(E) = ρ(E−θ)/(1−θ), then multiplied by what AI does to them, ℓ(E). Three numbers shape ℓ:

  • Novice Gain (gn, aclNoviceGain) — the output gain AI gives weaker performers on work inside its frontier.
  • Expert Gain (gx, aclExpertGain) — the same, for stronger performers. Smaller: AI levels the field rather than lifting it evenly.
  • Novice Deficit (dn, aclNoviceDeficit) — the output lost outside the frontier, by people who cannot tell the AI's work is wrong.

All three come from a single study of one profession on one model generation. This page asks which values of them leave AI ahead of the no-AI arm, and whether that answer survives the other assumption in w: ρ, swept from 60 to 800.

ρ is not a multiplier applied to everyone. It sits under a fractional exponent, so the same change in it moves people by very different amounts: going from ρ 60 to 800 multiplies what a person at the ceiling is worth by 13.3×, but someone at E = 0.8 by only 3.8× and someone at E = 0.65 by 1.5×. It sets the curvature of the expertise-to-value conversion, not its scale. The figures use the parameter names; the symbols above are the paper's notation for the same numbers.

The three numbers never touch a career — they are read off the population at the end of the run, never fed back into it. That is what makes this page cheap, and it is also why one run answers for several published configurations at once: the ACL family's fifteen configs differ only in these three parameters and in aiDampeningAbove, which does change how people learn. So the fifteen are five simulations read out at three parameter points. All three are marked on the figures below and pinned exactly onto the grid; acl.5.json is the one this run was taken under, at (0.43, 0.17, 0.19).

How to read it

What ρ does. Capability counts people in threshold-expert equivalents through w(E) = ρ(E−θ)/(1−θ). Everyone at the expert threshold counts 1 at every ρ; the person at the ceiling counts ρ; everyone in between counts ρ raised to a fraction. So ρ sets how steeply value rises across the distribution, and it does not convert into a single multiple: at ρ 800 a person at E = 0.8 is worth 32 threshold experts and one at E = 0.9 is worth 160, against 8.3 and 22 at ρ 60. It is a stated assumption, never measured, and it is swept here for exactly that reason. Nothing about the simulated careers changes with it — only how their expertise is added up.

Everything plotted is the median across the 18 AI-level × dampening cells of this experiment, each compared against its own no-AI arm. Both arms start from the same populations and the same seeds, so the difference between them is what AI did and nothing else. Two effects are in that difference and they pull opposite ways: the treatment arm has already lost expertise to AI-dampened learning before any leverage is applied, and the leverage then has to earn that back. A surface above zero means it did.

Verification

Passed.
